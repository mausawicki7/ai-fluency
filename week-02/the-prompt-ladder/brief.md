Details

Why it matters: The gap between a lazy prompt and an engineered one is the cheapest performance upgrade in AI, and most people never see it because they change five things at once and learn nothing. This assignment forces the discipline: one change at a time, output compared at every step. By the end you won't just have a better prompt, you'll know exactly which ingredient earned its place.

The task:

    Start with a genuinely weak prompt from your own track, the kind you would have typed a month ago. Something like "Help me with marketing," "Explain this data," "Build a website," "Review this design," "Write backend code," or "Summarize this article." Run it and save the output. This is your baseline, and it should be a little embarrassing.
    Improve it across five versions. Each version adds exactly one layer, and you pick the layer that attacks the current output's biggest weakness. Choose from: a clearer goal, a defined audience, real context, a specified output format, constraints, examples of what good looks like, quality criteria, review instructions, stated assumptions, or verification requirements. One layer per version, no bundling. If you can't say which single change a version makes, the version doesn't count.
    Run every version and compare its output against the previous one. Don't trust your memory, put them side by side.
    For each version, write four short notes: what changed in the prompt, what actually improved in the output, what still failed, and what you would try next. The "what improved in the output" note is the one that matters. "I added an audience" describes the prompt. "It stopped explaining basics my reader already knows" describes the result. We're grading the second kind.
    Finish with your final best prompt, cleaned up so someone else on your track could take it and use it without you in the room.

Deliverable: A prompt ladder document containing the weak baseline plus five versions, the output (or a representative excerpt) for each, your four notes per version, and the final reusable prompt.

Pass / revise:

    Six runs total (baseline plus five), each version tied to exactly one named layer
    Notes describe changes in the output , not just changes in the prompt
    At least one honest "this didn't help" or "this made it worse" moment. If all five versions improved things smoothly, you weren't looking hard enough
    The final prompt works for a stranger, not just for you
